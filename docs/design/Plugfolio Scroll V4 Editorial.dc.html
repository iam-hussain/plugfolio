Issue 01 — Aug 2026
An essay in four chapters

The Honest
Storefront.

On what happens when a creator’s page stops pretending to be a mall — and starts telling the truth about every button on it.
I — The link is broken II — A page, not a platform III — Money we don't touch IV — A coupon, printed honestly
I
Chapter one

The link in bio is broken.

Every night, under every good post, the same forty-seven comments arrive: link pls. The creator answers a dozen, gives up, and pins a bio link that leads to… a list of links. The shopper taps through three pages, loses the thread, and buys nothing. Everyone did everything right, and it still didn’t work.

The problem was never enthusiasm. It was plumbing. The post and the product live in different buildings, and the shopper is asked to walk between them — usually through a signup form.

Plugfolio’s answer is small and stubborn: put the products back inside the post, and never put a door in front of a person holding money.

II
Chapter two

A page, not a platform.

One creator, one page, one link. The posts import themselves from Instagram or YouTube; the creator points at the things in them and names a price. That’s the whole shop.

The radical part is who gets in: everyone, immediately. A visitor can browse, tap and buy without ever making an account. The optional account exists for exactly two verbs — follow and comment — and nothing else is behind it.

The same page shows four faces: to a stranger, to a signed-in follower, to a business scouting a collab, to the owner with edit tools. But the buy path is identical in all four. No viewer is ever the wrong kind of viewer.

III
Chapter three

Money we don’t touch.

There is no cart on Plugfolio, and no checkout, because there is no till. Every Buy button is a polite exit: it hands the shopper to the retailer’s own store, where the retailer’s own checkout does what it has always done.

The creator is credited by that retailer through an affiliate link — not by the shopper, and not by us. When a brand and a creator strike a deal in a collab thread, the payment settles off-platform too. We are, on purpose, the least interesting part of the transaction.

“We never see your card. The only thing we measure is the tap out.”

IV
Chapter four

A coupon, printed honestly.

A deal is the most persuasive sentence on any card, which is exactly why it must not lie. On Plugfolio a coupon is not a third kind of product — it rides on a normal card. Tap the code and it copies; the chip says Copied ✓ right where you tapped it.

Some codes work at a counter instead of a checkout — those say so, plainly, and their copies are labeled redemption not tracked in the creator’s own numbers. And when an offer expires, it doesn’t vanish: it folds into a quiet “Offer ended” and the product goes on being a product.

The storefront is honest.
